# Commercial Insurance Renewal Strategy Builder

## Purpose

Develop a forward-looking commercial insurance renewal strategy that improves the client’s negotiating position, program structure, coverage, and total cost of risk before the renewal becomes a last-minute remarketing exercise.

This Skill is designed to help the insurance professional:

- Move beyond automatic remarketing
- Identify multiple viable renewal pathways
- Evaluate whether the incumbent program should be retained, restructured, or marketed
- Identify realistic cost-control opportunities
- Identify coverage improvements or structural changes
- Develop carrier negotiation angles
- Determine whether alternative markets or program structures are worth evaluating
- Build a renewal strategy early enough to execute it
- Prioritize actions based on client impact, feasibility, and timing

The objective is **not** to generate a generic list of renewal ideas.

The objective is to create an executable **Renewal Strategy Brief** that helps the producer advise the client approximately 90 days before renewal with a clear point of view and multiple practical options.

---

# Core Outcome

A successful use of this Skill should allow the producer to answer:

- What is our best renewal strategy?
- Should we stay with the incumbent, negotiate, restructure, or remarket?
- What alternatives are genuinely viable?
- What can we do to improve leverage before approaching carriers?
- Where can cost be controlled without creating unacceptable coverage risk?
- Where should coverage be strengthened?
- What should we ask the incumbent carrier to change?
- Which markets or structures deserve consideration?
- What information must be developed before the renewal can be negotiated effectively?
- What should happen in the next 30, 60, and 90 days?

The producer should finish with a **decision framework and execution plan**, not merely a list of markets.

# Market Currency Standard

Renewal strategy is time-sensitive.

Any statement about:

- Current rate trends
- Carrier appetite
- Reinsurance conditions
- Capacity
- Catastrophe pricing
- Underwriting behavior
- Market hardening or softening
- Current carrier strategy
- Regulatory conditions

should be based on current information whenever research capabilities are available.

Do not treat old market commentary as current.

When current market data is unavailable:

- State that the market assumption is provisional
- Identify the assumption
- Explain what should be verified with current carrier or market intelligence
- Avoid presenting stale market conditions as fact

---

# Time Horizon Standard

Always determine how much time remains before renewal.

Classify the renewal window:

### 120+ Days
Full strategic flexibility remains.

### 90–119 Days
Ideal strategy window.

### 60–89 Days
Execution window is narrowing; prioritize high-impact actions.

### 30–59 Days
Focus on executable negotiation, selective marketing, and urgent data gaps.

### Under 30 Days
Avoid strategies that cannot realistically be completed before renewal.

The closer the renewal date, the more heavily feasibility should influence recommendations.

---

# Source Hierarchy

When multiple sources are available, prioritize:

1. Current policy documents
2. Current carrier-issued renewal information
3. Current loss and exposure data
4. Current carrier or market feedback
5. Agency-verified information
6. Client-provided information
7. Current authoritative market intelligence
8. Historical program information
9. Industry norms and professional inference

When sources conflict:

- Identify the discrepancy
- Do not silently choose one
- Use the most authoritative and current source available
- Flag material conflicts for human review

# Evidence Classification

Every material strategic observation should be classified when useful.

## Documented Fact

Supported by current policy, loss, exposure, or carrier information.

Example:

> The incumbent carrier has proposed a 14% property rate increase.

---

## Market-Supported Observation

Supported by current market intelligence or carrier feedback.

Example:

> Comparable habitational property accounts are continuing to experience constrained catastrophe capacity in this geography.

---

## Reasonable Strategic Inference

A likely conclusion based on the available account information and insurance-market behavior.

Example:

> Given three years of favorable loss performance, the incumbent may have room to improve its initial renewal position if credible alternative-market pressure exists.

---

## Strategy Hypothesis

A possible renewal approach worth testing.

Example:

> Increasing the property deductible may create meaningful premium relief without materially changing the client’s retained-risk tolerance.

---

## Requires Validation

An idea that cannot be responsibly recommended until additional information is obtained.

Example:

> A higher umbrella attachment point may reduce cost, but underlying contractual requirements must be reviewed first.

---

# Confidence Classification

Where useful, classify strategic conclusions:

### High Confidence
Supported by current account data, carrier information, or authoritative market information.

### Moderate Confidence
Supported by several indicators but requires validation.

### Low Confidence
Potentially viable but highly dependent on missing information or market confirmation.

Avoid false precision.

---

# Core Principle

## Do Not Default to Remarketing

Remarketing is one strategy—not the strategy.

Before recommending a market exercise, evaluate whether the client’s position may be improved through:

- Incumbent negotiation
- Program restructuring
- Exposure cleanup
- Deductible changes
- Limit changes
- Coverage refinement
- Loss-control improvement
- Better underwriting presentation
- Alternative markets
- Alternative program structures
- Multi-year strategy

The goal is to create leverage and improve outcomes—not simply generate more quotes.

# Workflow

Follow the workflow in order unless circumstances clearly justify another sequence.

---

## Step 1 — Establish the Renewal Position

Identify:

- Renewal date
- Days until renewal
- Current carrier
- Current premium
- Current program structure
- Current limits
- Current deductibles
- Current retentions
- Current coverage concerns
- Current client concerns
- Known renewal indications
- Known carrier concerns
- Relationship history
- Prior-year renewal outcome
- Prior-year negotiation strategy

Determine whether the account is currently:

- Stable
- Under pricing pressure
- Underwriting-challenged
- Loss-driven
- Capacity-constrained
- Coverage-deficient
- Service-challenged
- Strategically underdeveloped

---

## Step 2 — Establish the Client's Renewal Priorities

Determine what the client values most.

Potential priorities:

- Premium stability
- Lowest possible cost
- Coverage breadth
- Higher limits
- Lower deductibles
- Greater predictability
- Carrier quality
- Claims service
- Contract compliance
- Cash-flow management
- Long-term rate stability
- Risk retention
- Simplicity
- Loss-control support

Do not assume lower premium is always the primary objective.

If client priorities are unknown, identify this as an early discovery requirement.

---

## Step 3 — Review Loss Performance

Evaluate:

- Loss frequency
- Loss severity
- Open claims
- Large claims
- Trend
- Loss development
- Experience modification
- Preventable losses
- Catastrophic losses
- Claims management
- Reserve concerns
- Subrogation opportunities
- Return-to-work performance
- Loss-control activity

Ask:

> What story will the underwriter see when reviewing this account?

Then ask:

> What story should we be prepared to tell?

---

## Step 4 — Review Exposure Changes

Identify material changes since the prior renewal.

Examples:

- Revenue growth
- Payroll growth
- New locations
- New vehicles
- New contracts
- New products
- Geographic expansion
- Acquisitions
- New operations
- New equipment
- Property-value increases
- Cyber exposure changes
- Workforce changes
- International expansion
- Subcontractor changes
- New catastrophe exposure

Distinguish:

### Exposure-Driven Premium Change

Increase caused by growth or higher values.

### Rate-Driven Premium Change

Increase caused by pricing per unit of exposure.

This distinction is critical to renewal strategy.

---

## Step 5 — Identify Underwriting Friction

Determine what could weaken the client’s negotiating position.

Examples:

- Poor loss history
- Incomplete applications
- Late submissions
- Missing valuations
- Open loss-control recommendations
- Driver issues
- Weak cyber controls
- Poor contract controls
- Property deficiencies
- High-hazard operations
- Outdated schedules
- Inaccurate classifications
- Unresolved claims
- Catastrophe concentration

Rank issues by whether they can be improved before renewal.

---

## Step 6 — Identify Negotiating Strengths

Identify positive factors that should be emphasized.

Examples:

- Strong loss history
- Long carrier tenure
- Management stability
- Improved controls
- Safety improvements
- Fleet improvements
- Cybersecurity improvements
- Favorable claim closure
- Strong financials
- Risk-control investment
- Stable operations
- High retention potential
- Good payment history
- Growth
- Diversification
- Improved contractual controls

These should become part of the carrier narrative.

---

## Step 7 — Establish the Incumbent Strategy

Before marketing, determine what can be achieved with the incumbent.

Evaluate:

- Relationship strength
- Historical pricing
- Loss performance
- Carrier profitability
- Account tenure
- Current carrier appetite
- Competitive pressure
- Coverage flexibility
- Deductible options
- Multi-year potential
- Loss-control commitments
- Packaging opportunities
- Cross-line leverage

Develop specific incumbent asks.

Examples:

- Reduce rate increase
- Hold specific lines flat
- Improve umbrella pricing
- Increase limits without proportional premium
- Add or broaden endorsements
- Reduce restrictive exclusions
- Offer deductible alternatives
- Provide rate guarantee
- Offer multi-year structure
- Provide loss-control credits
- Improve payment terms

---

## Step 8 — Determine Whether Remarketing Is Justified

Evaluate:

### Reasons to Market

- Incumbent pricing is materially uncompetitive
- Carrier appetite is deteriorating
- Coverage restrictions are unacceptable
- Service has deteriorated
- Better alternatives are realistically available
- Program structure no longer fits
- Capacity is insufficient
- Client strategy requires change

### Reasons Not to Market

- Incumbent position is competitive
- Market alternatives are unlikely to improve the outcome
- Loss history is unfavorable
- Renewal timeline is too short
- Repeated annual marketing may damage carrier relationships
- Data quality is weak
- Client is unlikely to move
- Marketing would create noise without leverage

Do not recommend a full market exercise without a strategic reason.

---

## Step 9 — Develop Multiple Renewal Strategies

Create at least 2 and usually 3–5 viable approaches.

Potential strategies:

### Strategy A — Incumbent Optimization

Stay with the current carrier and negotiate aggressively.

### Strategy B — Selective Market Check

Approach a limited number of strategically chosen alternatives.

### Strategy C — Full Remarketing

Conduct a broad market exercise where justified.

### Strategy D — Program Restructure

Change limits, deductibles, attachment points, coverage structure, or packaging.

### Strategy E — Increased Risk Retention

Use higher deductibles or self-insured retention where financially appropriate.

### Strategy F — Alternative Risk Financing

Evaluate captive, group captive, loss-sensitive, deductible, retrospective, or other structures where applicable.

### Strategy G — Multi-Year Stabilization

Pursue rate guarantees, multi-year terms, or long-term carrier strategy where available.

Do not include strategies that are not operationally realistic.

## Step 10 — Identify Cost-Control Opportunities

Evaluate realistic cost levers.

Potential examples:

- Higher deductibles
- Higher retentions
- Deductible corridor
- Aggregate deductible
- Exposure accuracy
- Classification cleanup
- Property valuation accuracy
- Schedule cleanup
- Claims closure
- Reserve review
- Experience-mod improvement
- Loss-control improvements
- Fleet controls
- Cyber controls
- Program packaging
- Layer restructuring
- Umbrella attachment changes
- Alternative payment structures
- Premium financing
- Multi-year structures
- Alternative-risk financing
- Market competition

Do not recommend cost reduction if it creates disproportionate risk.

---

# Cost-Control Decision Rule

For every cost-control idea, evaluate:

1. Premium impact
2. Risk retained by client
3. Balance-sheet ability to absorb loss
4. Claims frequency
5. Contractual requirements
6. Client risk tolerance
7. Coverage trade-offs
8. Administrative complexity

A lower premium is not automatically a better outcome.

---

## Step 11 — Identify Coverage Enhancement Opportunities

Evaluate whether the renewal creates an opportunity to improve protection.

Potential areas:

- Higher umbrella/excess limits
- Business-income improvement
- Ordinance or law
- Equipment breakdown
- Cyber
- Social engineering
- Crime
- EPLI
- D&O
- Pollution
- Professional liability
- Product recall
- Inland marine
- Hired/non-owned auto
- Additional insured wording
- Contractual liability
- Property valuation
- Catastrophe coverage
- Contingent business interruption
- Extended period of indemnity

Only recommend enhancements tied to actual or likely exposure.

---

## Step 12 — Identify Structural Opportunities

Evaluate whether the program itself should change.

Potential structures:

- Package vs. monoline
- Primary + umbrella
- Layered excess
- Higher primary limits
- Lower primary / higher excess
- Deductible program
- Self-insured retention
- Loss-sensitive program
- Group captive
- Single-parent captive
- Rent-a-captive
- Retrospective rating
- Large-deductible program
- Alternative collateral arrangements
- Parametric coverage where appropriate
- Specialty program placement

Do not introduce sophisticated structures merely because they exist.

Tie them to client size, risk tolerance, loss performance, and feasibility.

---

## Step 13 — Develop Carrier Negotiation Angles

Create specific negotiating arguments.

Potential leverage may come from:

- Favorable loss performance
- Improved controls
- Long-term relationship
- Account profitability
- Competing market interest
- Portfolio relationship
- Exposure reduction
- Claim closure
- Better underwriting information
- Strong management
- Client investment in risk control
- Multi-line opportunity
- Growth opportunity
- Commitment to carrier partnership

Avoid weak negotiation language such as:

> Please sharpen the pencil.

Instead use a factual case.

Example:

> The account has produced three consecutive years below the carrier’s target loss ratio, has implemented telematics across the fleet, and has closed two legacy claims. We are requesting that the initial auto increase be reduced from 15% to 5% or less.

---

## Step 14 — Improve the Underwriting Story

Identify information that can improve carrier perception.

Potential improvements:

- Executive summary
- Narrative explaining losses
- Corrective-action documentation
- Safety program
- Return-to-work program
- Fleet controls
- Cyber controls
- Financial strength
- Growth story
- Property improvements
- Contract controls
- Claims management
- Loss-control response
- Management biography

Ask:

> What information would make an underwriter more comfortable writing this account?

Then incorporate it into the renewal plan.

---

## Step 15 — Evaluate Market Approach

If marketing is warranted, determine:

- Which markets should be approached
- Why each market fits
- Which markets should not be approached
- Whether direct or wholesale access is needed
- Whether program markets exist
- Whether capacity is available
- Whether market overlap should be controlled
- Whether the incumbent should receive a last-look opportunity

Avoid indiscriminate market blocking.

The objective is quality of market approach, not number of submissions.

---

## Step 16 — Consider Timing and Execution Risk

For each recommendation, ask:

- Can this realistically be completed before renewal?
- Does it require new underwriting information?
- Does it require client approval?
- Does it require carrier loss control?
- Does it require legal review?
- Does it require actuarial analysis?
- Does it require collateral?
- Does it require new financial information?
- Does it require executive decision-making?

Do not recommend a strategy that cannot reasonably be executed in the available time.

---

## Step 17 — Build the 90-Day Renewal Plan

When approximately 90 days remain, structure:

### Days 90–75 — Diagnose

- Confirm client priorities
- Review losses
- Review exposure changes
- Review coverage
- Identify underwriting weaknesses
- Build renewal strategy
- Determine market approach

### Days 74–60 — Prepare

- Complete exposure data
- Close information gaps
- Build underwriting narrative
- Address loss-control issues
- Develop carrier asks
- Finalize marketing strategy

### Days 59–30 — Execute

- Negotiate incumbent
- Approach selected markets
- Evaluate alternatives
- Model cost/coverage options
- Begin recommendation analysis

### Days 29–15 — Decide

- Compare options
- Negotiate final terms
- Resolve coverage issues
- Present recommendation
- Obtain client direction

### Days 14–0 — Bind & Confirm

- Finalize coverage
- Complete binding requirements
- Confirm financing/payment
- Verify policy instructions
- Document decisions

Adjust the timeline based on actual days remaining.

---

## Step 18 — Define Contingency Strategies

Every renewal plan should identify what happens if the primary strategy fails.

Examples:

### Primary
Incumbent reduces increase to target.

### Contingency
Move selected lines to alternative carrier.

### Backstop
Renew incumbent temporarily while continuing longer-term restructuring where permissible.

Contingency planning reduces last-minute decisions.

---

## Step 19 — Rank the Renewal Options

Prioritize each strategy based on:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Client Value | Does it materially improve the client’s position? |
| Cost Impact | Could it improve total cost? |
| Coverage Impact | Does it strengthen or weaken protection? |
| Stability | Does it improve predictability? |
| Feasibility | Can it actually be executed? |
| Market Support | Are carriers likely to support it? |
| Timing | Is there sufficient time? |
| Complexity | Is the administrative burden reasonable? |
| Risk Retention | Does it shift material risk back to the client? |

Use professional judgment rather than arbitrary numerical scoring unless requested.

---

# Priority Classification

### Priority 1 — Primary Strategy

Best combination of client value, feasibility, and expected outcome.

### Priority 2 — Strong Alternative

Viable if the primary approach does not achieve acceptable results.

### Priority 3 — Contingency

Useful fallback or secondary option.

### Not Recommended

Strategically possible but not justified under current conditions.

---

## Step 20 — Develop the Client Decision Framework

The client should be able to compare renewal options across:

- Premium
- Coverage
- Deductible / retention
- Limits
- Carrier
- Stability
- Claims implications
- Administrative burden
- Contract compliance
- Risk retained
- Long-term strategy

Do not reduce the decision to premium alone.

---

# Decision Rules

## Strategy Before Marketing

Do not begin with:

> Which carriers should we quote?

Begin with:

> What outcome are we trying to create?

Then determine whether marketing supports that outcome.

---

## Separate Exposure Growth From Rate Increase

When premium increases, identify whether the cause is:

- More exposure
- Higher rate
- Coverage change
- Limit increase
- Loss performance
- Catastrophe load
- Market condition
- Combination of factors

This distinction improves client communication and carrier negotiation.

---

## Cost Reduction Must Be Risk-Aware

Never recommend reducing premium without identifying:

- Coverage reduction
- Higher retained risk
- Higher deductible
- Lower limit
- Narrower wording
- Carrier trade-off

where applicable.

---

## Coverage Improvement Must Be Exposure-Driven

Do not recommend additional coverage merely because it exists.

Tie the recommendation to:

- Actual operations
- Contractual requirements
- Severity
- Emerging exposure
- Client goals

---

## Current Market Intelligence Over Historical Assumptions

When current market conditions matter, use current information where available.

Do not rely on last year’s market environment.

---

## Feasibility Over Creativity

A creative idea that cannot be implemented before renewal is not a useful renewal strategy.

---

## Multiple Viable Paths

Unless circumstances strongly support only one outcome, provide multiple strategies.

The client should understand:

- Primary strategy
- Alternative strategy
- Contingency strategy

---

# Materiality / Prioritization Framework

Prioritize recommendations using:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Financial Impact | How much could this affect total cost? |
| Coverage Impact | Could protection materially improve or deteriorate? |
| Severity | Could the issue create a major uninsured loss? |
| Stability | Does it improve long-term predictability? |
| Execution Risk | How difficult is implementation? |
| Timing | Can it be completed before renewal? |
| Carrier Appetite | Is market support realistic? |
| Client Fit | Does it align with risk tolerance and goals? |
| Evidence | How strongly is the strategy supported? |

Avoid false precision.

# Failure Conditions

The Skill should not be considered complete if:

- The strategy defaults to remarketing without analysis
- Only one option is provided when reasonable alternatives exist
- Cost savings are recommended without identifying risk trade-offs
- Coverage enhancements are generic or product-driven
- Current market claims are presented without current support
- Historical market assumptions are treated as current
- Strategies cannot realistically be executed before renewal
- Loss performance is ignored
- Exposure changes are ignored
- Carrier negotiation angles are vague
- The underwriting story is not addressed
- No contingency strategy is provided
- No clear primary recommendation is made
- Assumptions are presented as facts

If any failure condition exists, revise before finalizing.

# Guardrails

## Do Not Guarantee Renewal Outcomes

Never guarantee:

- Rate reduction
- Premium savings
- Carrier acceptance
- Coverage availability
- Renewal terms
- Capacity
- Multi-year pricing
- Alternative-program feasibility

---

## Do Not Invent Market Conditions

Do not fabricate:

- Current rate trends
- Carrier appetite
- Market capacity
- Reinsurance conditions
- Carrier underwriting positions
- Regulatory changes

If current information is unavailable, label assumptions accordingly.

---

## Do Not Fabricate Program Information

Never invent:

- Premium
- Limits
- Deductibles
- Losses
- Exposures
- Carriers
- Terms
- Claims
- Endorsements
- Coverage

---

## Do Not Sacrifice Coverage Without Disclosure

If a cost-control strategy reduces protection, increases retention, or changes risk transfer:

- State the trade-off clearly
- Explain the potential client impact
- Require professional and client review

---

## Do Not Treat Alternative Risk Financing as Automatically Better

Captives, loss-sensitive structures, deductibles, and other alternatives may require:

- Sufficient scale
- Favorable loss performance
- Financial strength
- Collateral
- Risk tolerance
- Administrative capability
- Long-term commitment

Recommend additional analysis when appropriate.

---

## No Legal Advice

Contractual, regulatory, or legal issues may be identified.

Do not provide unsupported legal conclusions.

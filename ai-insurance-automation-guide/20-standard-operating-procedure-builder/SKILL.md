# Insurance Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) Builder

## Purpose

Convert a real insurance or agency process into a clear, structured, executable Standard Operating Procedure that can be used for training, onboarding, quality control, workflow automation, and consistent team execution.

This Skill is designed to help the insurance professional:

- Turn informal process knowledge into a repeatable workflow
- Define realistic sequencing
- Clarify roles and responsibilities
- Identify handoffs between people, teams, systems, and carriers
- Define required inputs and prerequisites
- Establish checkpoints and quality-control steps
- Identify common failure points
- Define exceptions and escalation paths
- Establish timing expectations
- Define workflow status
- Define completion criteria
- Improve consistency across teams
- Support future automation by AI Teammates

The objective is **not** to create a theoretical process map.

The objective is to document:

> How the work should actually be performed, who owns each step, what must be checked, and what must be true before the process is complete.

---

# Core Outcome

A successful use of this Skill should allow a new team member to understand:

- When the process begins
- What triggers it
- What information is required
- What systems or documents are used
- What steps happen and in what order
- Who owns each step
- Where handoffs occur
- What deadlines or service standards apply
- What quality checks are required
- What exceptions may occur
- When escalation is required
- What common mistakes to avoid
- When the workflow is officially complete

The SOP should be usable without relying on tribal knowledge.

# Document Completeness Standard

Where process documentation is supplied, classify it as:

### Complete Enough

The available information reasonably supports a usable SOP.

### Partially Complete

A draft SOP can be created, but important workflow details require confirmation.

### Fragmentary

The available description is too limited to produce a reliable SOP.

When information is incomplete:

- Draft only what is supportable
- Identify unresolved workflow questions
- Label assumptions
- Do not invent hidden approvals or system steps

---

# Source Hierarchy

When instructions conflict, prioritize:

1. Governing law or regulation
2. Current carrier or contractual requirement
3. Approved agency policy
4. Current system requirement
5. Process-owner instruction
6. Team operating practice
7. Historical SOP
8. Professional inference

Material conflicts should be escalated.

- Carrier portals

- Vendor capabilities
- System capabilities
- Technology workflows
- Compliance requirements

# Jurisdiction / Governing Authority Standard

Where applicable, identify:

- State
- Carrier
- Regulator
- Rating authority
- Policy
- Contract
- Benefit plan
- Agency agreement
- Internal compliance standard

A procedure should not apply universally if the governing requirement is jurisdiction-specific.

---

# Authority vs. Inference Standard

Distinguish:

### Governing Requirement

Required by law, carrier, contract, or approved agency policy.

### Current Operating Practice

How the team presently performs the work.

### Recommended Improvement

A proposed workflow enhancement.

### AI Inference

A process assumption derived from incomplete information.

### Decision Authority

The person or organization authorized to approve the SOP.

AI-generated process design is not automatically an approved operating procedure.

---

# Evidence Classification

Process elements should be treated as one of the following where useful.

## Confirmed Process Step

Explicitly supported by the provided process.

## Confirmed Requirement

Required by governing documentation.

## Current Practice

Described as existing team behavior.

## Recommended Control

Suggested to improve reliability.

## Assumption

Necessary to complete the draft but not confirmed.

## Requires Process Owner Confirmation

Material workflow detail remains unresolved.

---

# Confidence Classification

Where useful:

### High Confidence

The process step or requirement is directly supported.

### Moderate Confidence

Likely correct but dependent on a missing detail.

### Low Confidence

Substantial workflow uncertainty remains.

# Core Principle

## Document the Work as It Actually Happens

A useful SOP should reflect:

- Real sequencing
- Real dependencies
- Real systems
- Real handoffs
- Real quality checks
- Real exceptions

Avoid idealized workflows that cannot be followed operationally.

---

# Execution Readiness Standard

Before approving the SOP, classify it as:

### Ready for Use

Core steps, ownership, systems, controls, and completion criteria are confirmed.

### Conditionally Ready

The SOP is usable, but one or more material items require confirmation.

### Not Ready

Critical workflow, authority, or system information is missing.

Do not label a draft SOP operationally ready merely because it is well formatted.

- Completion target

Avoid timelines that depend on third-party performance unless identified as estimates.

# Workflow

## Step 1 — Define the Process Trigger

Identify exactly what starts the process.

Examples:

- Client request received
- Renewal reaches 120 days
- Carrier notice received
- New employee hired
- Policy issued
- Claim reported
- Statement received
- Application completed

Avoid vague triggers such as:

> When needed.

---

## Step 2 — Define the Desired Outcome

State what the process is designed to accomplish.

Example:

> The vehicle endorsement process is complete when the carrier has processed the change, updated documentation has been received, the client has been notified, and the agency system has been updated.

The desired outcome should drive completion criteria.

---

## Step 3 — Identify Required Inputs

Document what must be available before work begins.

Examples:

- Policy number
- Client authorization
- Effective date
- VIN
- Driver information
- Application
- Supporting documents
- Signed form
- Carrier requirement
- Current policy data

---

## Step 4 — Identify Roles

List every role involved.

Potential roles:

- Producer
- Account Executive
- Account Manager
- CSR
- Claims Advocate
- Accounting
- Compliance
- Carrier
- Underwriter
- Client
- Vendor
- AI Teammate

Avoid naming specific employees unless the SOP is intentionally person-specific.

Prefer role-based ownership.

---

## Step 5 — Identify Systems

Document systems used.

Examples:

- AMS
- CRM
- Carrier portal
- Email
- Accounting platform
- Document management
- Client portal
- Quoting platform
- AI Teammate
- Ticketing system

Include where data must be entered or stored.

---

## Step 6 — Establish the Starting Validation

Before work begins, verify:

- Correct client
- Correct policy
- Correct effective date
- Required authority
- Required documents
- Required information
- Duplicate request status
- Existing open workflow

Do not begin irreversible action until critical prerequisites are validated.

---

# Pre-Execution Validation Standard

For operational processes, validate:

1. Correct entity
2. Correct request
3. Correct authority
4. Required information
5. Duplicate / existing transaction
6. Effective date
7. Applicable governing requirement

This helps prevent avoidable workflow errors.

---

## Step 7 — Break the Process Into Logical Steps

Each step should include:

### Step Number

Sequential order.

### Action

What must be done.

### Owner

Who performs it.

### System / Tool

Where it occurs.

### Required Input

What is needed.

### Output

What should result.

### Timing

When it should occur.

### Checkpoint

What must be verified.

Avoid steps that combine multiple unrelated actions.

---

## Step 8 — Define Handoffs

Identify every point where ownership moves.

For each handoff, define:

- Sending role
- Receiving role
- What is being transferred
- Required information
- Status at handoff
- Confirmation method

Example:

> Account Manager submits the endorsement request to the carrier and changes the case status to Waiting on Carrier.

---

# Handoff Integrity Standard

A handoff is not complete simply because information was sent.

A valid handoff should confirm:

- Correct recipient
- Complete information
- Clear requested action
- Current status
- Required timing
- Follow-up ownership

Poor handoffs create hidden work queues.

---

## Step 9 — Define Decision Points

Identify where the workflow branches.

Example:

```text
If carrier approval is not required:
    Process within agency authority.
Else:
    Submit to carrier and move status to Waiting on Carrier.
```

Decision points should be explicit.

---

## Step 10 — Define Quality-Control Checkpoints

Identify checks before the process moves forward.

Potential QC checks:

- Correct policy
- Correct client
- Correct effective date
- Correct limit
- Correct document
- Correct carrier
- Complete data
- Required signature
- Correct billing
- Correct commission
- Correct system update
- Client confirmation

QC should occur where an error is easiest to catch.

---

# Checkpoint Standard

Every material workflow should define at least one of:

### Input Check

Validate information before processing.

### Mid-Process Check

Validate critical action before advancing.

### Final Check

Confirm intended outcome before closing.

Do not rely solely on final inspection.

---

## Step 11 — Define Exceptions

Identify situations where the normal workflow should not continue.

Examples:

- Missing authority
- Coverage question
- Carrier declines
- Client request conflicts with policy
- Duplicate request
- System outage
- Missing document
- Deadline missed
- Compliance issue
- High-value transaction
- Unusual carrier requirement

---

## Step 12 — Define Escalation Rules

For each material exception, identify:

- Trigger
- Escalation owner
- Required response
- Deadline
- Decision authority

Example:

> Any request that may create a retroactive coverage change must be escalated to the Account Executive before submission.

---

# Human Review Escalation Standard

Require human review before:

- Binding coverage
- Cancelling coverage
- Coverage denial communication
- Material legal interpretation
- Regulatory response
- High-impact client communication
- Large financial adjustment
- Material E&O concern
- Irreversible system action
- Low-confidence exception handling

AI may support execution.

Authorized personnel retain decision responsibility.

---

## Step 13 — Define Client Communication

Where client communication is required, define:

- When acknowledgment is sent
- When action confirmation is sent
- When client input is needed
- When delays are communicated
- When final completion is communicated

Do not make clients chase the process for status.

---

## Step 14 — Define Update Cadence

For unresolved workflows, state when follow-up occurs.

Examples:

- Daily for urgent cases
- Within two business days
- At carrier response
- Before deadline
- Weekly during extended review

Use the cadence appropriate to the process.

---

# Update Cadence Standard

Every open material workflow should have either:

- Expected completion time

or

- Next-update commitment

Avoid indefinite open status.

---

## Step 15 — Define Internal Documentation

Specify what must be recorded.

Examples:

- Case note
- Activity log
- Policy record
- Client communication
- Carrier response
- Approval
- Attachment
- Claim number
- Transaction reference

Documentation should support future review and auditability.

---

## Step 16 — Define System-of-Record Updates

Identify which system is authoritative.

Examples:

- AMS
- CRM
- Accounting system
- Claims system
- HRIS
- Benefits platform

Do not leave final transaction data only in email.

---

# System-of-Record Standard

Where multiple systems are involved:

1. Identify the authoritative system
2. Define what must be updated
3. Define when updates occur
4. Avoid conflicting duplicate records

This is especially important for AI-driven workflows.

---

## Step 17 — Define Stop Rules

Specify when normal execution should stop.

Examples:

- Missing required authority
- Inconsistent information
- Coverage ambiguity
- Duplicate transaction
- Financial mismatch
- Carrier decline
- Regulatory concern
- System failure
- Required approval not received

Do not continue automatically through unresolved high-risk exceptions.

---

## Step 18 — Define Completion Criteria

State exactly what must be true before the process is closed.

Potential criteria:

- Action approved
- Carrier processed
- Documentation received
- System updated
- Client notified
- Accounting reconciled
- QC completed
- Required attachments stored
- Case status changed to Completed

Do not use:

> Done when submitted

unless submission is truly the intended final outcome.

---

# Completion Criteria Standard

Completion should be defined from the intended business outcome backward.

Example:

An endorsement workflow may be complete only when:

1. Carrier processes the endorsement
2. Revised document is received
3. Policy record is updated
4. Client receives confirmation
5. Case documentation is complete

---

# Data Integrity Standard

Where the SOP includes data handling, define controls for:

- Missing values
- Duplicate records
- Conflicting information
- Stale data
- Incorrect identifiers
- Invalid effective dates
- Incorrect totals
- Unreconciled transactions

Do not allow material data discrepancies to pass silently.

---

# Data Normalization Standard

Where relevant, define what may be standardized.

Examples:

- Date format
- State names
- Policy formatting
- Status labels
- Document naming

Do not silently alter material business data.

---

# Governance / Conflict Prevention Standard

Before execution, consider:

- Duplicate requests
- Existing cases
- Conflicting instructions
- Existing carrier submission
- Market blocking
- Existing endorsement
- Existing claim
- Existing approval
- Pending cancellation
- System-of-record conflict

Avoid duplicate or conflicting actions.

---

# Ownership Standard

Every material step should have a clear owner.

Use role-based ownership where possible.

Example:

| Step | Owner |
|---|---|
| Validate request | Account Manager |
| Approve exception | Account Executive |
| Process transaction | Carrier |
| Confirm final document | Account Manager |
| Update system | Account Manager / AI Teammate |

---

# Dependency Standard

Identify dependencies such as:

- Client response
- Carrier approval
- Underwriter response
- Compliance approval
- Vendor action
- System availability
- Documentation

For each dependency, define:

- Owner
- Expected timing
- Follow-up action
- Escalation trigger

---

# Stop Rules

The SOP workflow should stop or escalate when:

- Required information is missing
- Authority is unclear
- Governing documents conflict
- Duplicate action is detected
- Critical data does not reconcile
- Compliance concern exists
- Coverage interpretation is required
- Irreversible action exceeds user authority
- System failure prevents reliable processing

---

# Completion Criteria Standard

The SOP itself is complete when:

1. Trigger is defined
2. Desired outcome is defined
3. Required inputs are identified
4. Roles are identified
5. Systems are identified
6. Step sequence is complete
7. Handoffs are defined
8. Decision points are documented
9. QC checkpoints are included
10. Exceptions are documented
11. Escalation rules are defined
12. Timing is included
13. Ownership is clear
14. Documentation requirements are included
15. System-of-record requirements are identified
16. Stop rules are defined
17. Completion criteria are explicit

The operating workflow is complete only when the SOP's process-specific completion criteria are satisfied.

---

# Materiality / Prioritization Framework

Prioritize controls based on:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Coverage Impact | Could an error affect coverage? |
| Financial Impact | Could an error create financial loss? |
| Compliance Impact | Could a requirement be violated? |
| Client Impact | Could the client be harmed or inconvenienced? |
| Operational Impact | Could work stop or duplicate? |
| Reversibility | Can an error easily be corrected? |
| Frequency | How often does the process occur? |
| Error History | Is this a known breakdown point? |
| Dependency | Does another party control completion? |

More critical processes should have stronger controls.

# Guardrails

## Do Not Invent Workflow Requirements

Never fabricate:

- Carrier requirements
- Regulatory requirements
- Approval authority
- Client obligations
- System capabilities
- Timelines
- Service standards

---

## Do Not Preserve Broken Processes Uncritically

If the supplied process contains:

- Duplicate work
- Missing ownership
- Missing QC
- Unsafe practices
- Compliance risk
- Conflicting instructions

identify the issue rather than simply documenting it as best practice.

---

## Do Not Over-Automate High-Risk Decisions

AI may assist with:

- Intake
- Data validation
- Drafting
- Classification
- Routing
- Follow-up
- Documentation
- Reconciliation

AI should not independently perform unauthorized:

- Binding
- Cancellation
- Coverage determination
- Legal interpretation
- Financial approval
- Regulatory decision
- Claim settlement

---

## No Legal Advice

Legal or regulatory questions should be escalated to appropriate authority.

## 8. Key Quality-Control Checkpoints

### Intake Check

Confirm:

- VIN
- Vehicle
- Effective date
- Driver
- Lienholder

### Submission Check

Confirm the request matches the client's instructions before submitting.

### Final Endorsement Check

Confirm:

- Vehicle is correct
- Effective date is correct
- Driver information is correct where applicable
- Lienholder is correct
- Updated documentation matches the requested transaction

---

## 9. Common Errors / Breakdown Points

### Duplicate Submission

**Why it happens:**  
A second AM processes the request because an existing case was not checked.

**Risk:**  
Duplicate or conflicting carrier transactions.

**Prevention:**  
Search AMS for an existing open case before submission.

---

### Closing Case After Submission

**Why it happens:**  
Carrier submission is mistaken for completion.

**Risk:**  
Carrier may reject or process incorrectly without agency follow-up.

**Prevention:**  
Keep case open until final endorsement is reviewed and client documentation is delivered.

---

### Incorrect Effective Date

**Why it happens:**  
Date is assumed instead of confirmed.

**Risk:**  
Vehicle may not be added as intended.

**Prevention:**  
Effective date must be explicitly validated before submission.

---

## 10. Escalation

Escalate to the Service Manager when:

- Client requests retroactive change outside normal authority
- Carrier declines the requested change
- Coverage question arises
- Vehicle use creates underwriting concern
- Client needs immediate proof but carrier processing is delayed
- Requested transaction conflicts with existing policy information

# Commercial Insurance Client Situation Communication

## Purpose

Create clear, professional, empathetic client communications for important insurance situations that may involve uncertainty, disruption, cost, coverage concerns, claims, renewal issues, carrier decisions, compliance matters, service problems, or other material events.

This Skill is designed to help the insurance professional:

- Explain what is happening in plain language
- Clarify how the situation affects the client
- Separate known facts from unresolved issues
- Avoid unnecessary technical language
- Communicate difficult news without creating panic
- Provide practical next steps
- Preserve trust and confidence
- Acknowledge inconvenience or concern when appropriate
- Avoid overpromising outcomes
- Maintain a professional advisor role
- Create a clear path forward

The objective is **not** to make every situation sound positive.

The objective is to communicate:

> What happened, what it means, what we are doing, and what happens next.

---

# Core Outcome

A successful use of this Skill should leave the client understanding:

- What happened
- Why it matters
- What is known
- What is still unresolved
- How the situation affects them
- Whether they need to take action
- What the advisor or agency is doing
- What happens next
- When they should expect another update
- Who is responsible for each next step

The client should feel informed and supported without being given false reassurance.

# Preferred Source Documents

Where available, prioritize:

1. Current carrier communication
2. Current policy documents
3. Claim correspondence
4. Renewal proposal
5. Cancellation or non-renewal documentation
6. Billing records
7. Underwriting requests
8. Current agency case notes
9. Client-confirmed information
10. Internal notes

Do not describe an internal interpretation as though it were a carrier-confirmed fact.

---

# Source Hierarchy

When multiple sources conflict, prioritize:

1. Current authoritative carrier or governing documentation
2. Current policy or endorsement
3. Current claim or underwriting correspondence
4. Agency-verified information
5. Client-confirmed information
6. Internal notes
7. Professional inference

When sources conflict:

- Identify the conflict
- Do not silently reconcile it
- Avoid sending contradictory information to the client
- Resolve material discrepancies before final communication when possible

# Situation Status Standard

Before drafting, classify each material issue as:

## Confirmed

Supported by authoritative information.

## Pending

Known issue, but outcome is not yet determined.

## Proposed

A recommended next step or possible solution.

## Under Review

Actively being evaluated by carrier, agency, counsel, or another party.

## Requires Client Action

The client must provide information, make a decision, or complete an action.

## Requires Third-Party Action

Carrier, TPA, claims adjuster, vendor, regulator, or another party must act.

Do not blur these categories.

---

# Evidence Classification

Every material statement should be treated as one of the following.

## Confirmed Fact

Directly supported by current information.

Example:

> The carrier has requested updated driver information before completing renewal review.

---

## Advisor Interpretation

A professional explanation of what the confirmed fact means.

Example:

> This means the renewal cannot be finalized until the requested information is reviewed.

---

## Pending Outcome

A matter that remains unresolved.

Example:

> The carrier is still reviewing whether it will remove the new exclusion.

---

## Recommended Action

The advisor's proposed next step.

Example:

> We recommend providing the requested information immediately so the carrier can complete its review.

---

## Reassurance Based on Process

Confidence grounded in a specific action or plan.

Example:

> We have escalated the issue to the carrier's senior underwriting team and will continue following it until we receive a definitive response.

Avoid unsupported reassurance such as:

> Everything will be fine.

---

# Confidence Classification

Where useful, classify statements:

### High Confidence

Confirmed by authoritative information.

### Moderate Confidence

Strongly supported but dependent on another party or unresolved condition.

### Low Confidence

Tentative and should not be presented as a likely outcome without qualification.

---

# Core Principle

## Clarity Before Reassurance

Do not reassure the client before clearly explaining the situation.

A strong sequence is:

**Situation → Impact → Action → Guidance → Next Update**

The client should understand what is happening before being told what to feel about it.

---

# Workflow

Follow the workflow in order unless circumstances clearly require another sequence.

---

## Step 1 — Identify the Communication Objective

Determine why the message is being sent.

Examples:

- Inform
- Explain
- Request action
- Deliver difficult news
- Correct prior information
- Provide status update
- Escalate urgency
- Reassure through process
- Present alternatives
- Confirm resolution

The message should have one primary objective.

---

## Step 2 — Identify the Recipient Perspective

Ask:

- What does the client already know?
- What are they likely worried about?
- What could be confusing?
- What financial or operational impact matters most?
- What do they need to decide?
- What are they expecting from the advisor?

Write from the client’s perspective, not the agency’s internal process.

---

## Step 3 — Separate Facts From Internal Commentary

Remove internal terms such as:

- Escalation strategy
- Coverage problem
- Agency error
- Carrier issue
- Sales opportunity
- Retention concern
- E&O exposure
- Producer liability
- Internal blame
- Negotiation leverage

unless disclosure is necessary and appropriate.

Internal reasoning should not automatically appear in client-facing communication.

---

# Internal vs. External Information Boundary Standard

Before drafting, classify information as:

### Client-Appropriate

Information the client needs to understand or act.

### Internal Only

Agency strategy, speculation, sensitive commentary, blame analysis, liability concerns, or private notes.

### Requires Judgment

Information that may need disclosure but should be reviewed by an authorized professional first.

Do not leak internal reasoning into client-facing communication.

---

## Step 4 — Establish What Is Confirmed

Identify:

- What happened
- When
- Who communicated it
- What decision has been made
- What is documented
- What is not disputed

Lead with confirmed facts.

---

## Step 5 — Identify What Is Still Pending

Clearly distinguish unresolved matters.

Examples:

- Carrier review
- Claim investigation
- Underwriting approval
- Policy correction
- Premium adjustment
- Coverage determination
- Document processing
- Regulatory response

Use language such as:

> The carrier is currently reviewing...

not:

> The carrier should approve...

unless supported.

---

## Step 6 — Explain the Situation in Plain Language

Translate the issue into client-friendly language.

Avoid beginning with technical policy terms.

Example:

Instead of:

> The carrier has issued a conditional non-renewal pending receipt of loss-control compliance.

Prefer:

> The carrier has told us it will not finalize the renewal unless the requested safety items are completed and documented.

Then explain the technical detail if necessary.

---

## Step 7 — Explain Why It Happened

Where the reason is known, explain it.

Potential reasons:

- Loss experience
- Exposure change
- Underwriting requirement
- Carrier appetite
- Market conditions
- Missing information
- Audit
- Policy change
- Regulatory requirement
- Carrier processing issue

Do not guess.

If the reason has not been confirmed, say:

> We are still confirming the carrier's reasoning.

---

## Step 8 — Explain the Client Impact

Answer:

> What does this mean for the client?

Potential impacts:

- Additional premium
- Reduced coverage
- Delay
- Action required
- Coverage interruption risk
- Higher deductible
- Documentation requirement
- Operational requirement
- Claim uncertainty
- Renewal uncertainty
- No immediate impact

Do not make the client interpret the impact themselves.

---

# Impact Translation Standard

Translate technical events into practical consequences.

Example:

Technical event:

> Increase in wind/hail deductible to 5%.

Client impact:

> In a covered wind or hail loss, the business may now be responsible for a substantially larger portion of the loss before insurance begins paying, depending on how the percentage deductible applies.

The communication should answer:

> What changes for me?

---

## Step 9 — Distinguish Immediate vs. Potential Impact

Separate:

### Immediate Impact

What is already happening.

### Potential Impact

What could happen if the issue is not resolved.

Example:

> There is no interruption in coverage today. However, if the carrier does not receive the requested information by September 15, the renewal may not be finalized.

This distinction can materially reduce unnecessary client anxiety.

---

## Step 10 — Identify Client Action

If the client must act, explain:

- What is needed
- Why
- Deadline
- Format
- Where to send it
- What happens after receipt

Avoid vague requests.

Weak:

> Please send this ASAP.

Better:

> Please send the updated vehicle schedule by Friday so we can provide it to underwriting before the renewal decision deadline.

---

## Step 11 — Identify Agency Action

Explain what the advisor or agency is doing.

Examples:

- Escalating to senior underwriting
- Reviewing alternatives
- Correcting documentation
- Obtaining revised terms
- Coordinating with claims
- Reviewing policy wording
- Preparing a market alternative
- Monitoring deadline
- Following up with carrier

This reinforces accountability.

---

## Step 12 — Identify Third-Party Action

Where applicable, explain what must happen outside the agency.

Examples:

- Carrier approval
- Adjuster review
- Inspection
- TPA processing
- Regulatory approval
- Vendor response

Do not imply control over third parties.

---

# Ownership Standard

Every material next step should have an owner.

Potential owners:

- Client
- Agency
- Carrier
- Adjuster
- TPA
- Vendor
- Legal counsel
- Other party

Avoid ambiguous statements such as:

> We need to get this resolved.

Prefer:

> We have asked the carrier to review the endorsement, and we expect its response by Wednesday.

---

## Step 13 — Establish Timeline

Identify:

- Deadline
- Expected response
- Next update
- Renewal date
- Effective date
- Decision date
- Claim milestone

If timing is uncertain, say so.

Do not invent deadlines or response times.

---

# Update Cadence Standard

For unresolved important situations, establish when the client should hear from the advisor again.

Examples:

> We expect the carrier's response by Thursday and will update you that day even if the review is still pending.

or:

> We will follow up no later than Friday with either a resolution or a status update.

For unresolved material issues, silence can damage trust.

---

## Step 14 — Develop Reassurance Based on Action

Reassurance should be grounded in what is actually being done.

Good:

> We have already escalated this to the carrier's senior underwriting team and are also preparing an alternative option so we are not dependent on a single outcome.

Weak:

> Don't worry—we'll take care of it.

Avoid empty reassurance.

---

## Step 15 — Acknowledge Client Frustration or Concern

When appropriate, acknowledge:

- Inconvenience
- Uncertainty
- Timing
- Unexpected cost
- Operational disruption
- Complexity

Example:

> I understand this is frustrating, particularly this close to renewal.

Do not over-apologize when the agency did not cause the issue.

Do not accept fault without authority.

---

## Step 16 — Determine Whether an Apology Is Appropriate

Use an apology when:

- The agency caused a documented error
- Communication was delayed
- A commitment was missed
- The client experienced avoidable inconvenience caused by the agency

Do not:

- Admit legal liability
- Speculate about fault
- Blame individuals
- Make unauthorized admissions

Use appropriate professional language.

---

## Step 17 — Identify Alternatives

Where realistic, explain available alternatives.

Examples:

- Negotiate with incumbent
- Adjust deductible
- Market another carrier
- Provide missing information
- Complete loss-control requirements
- Obtain temporary solution
- Review alternate coverage
- Appeal carrier decision

Do not create alternatives that are not actually executable.

---

## Step 18 — Identify Recommendation

When enough information exists, state what the advisor recommends.

Example:

> Our recommendation is to complete the requested inspection items immediately while we continue negotiating the renewal and preparing a backup market option.

Be clear.

---

## Step 19 — Identify the CTA

Use one primary CTA whenever possible.

Examples:

- Send requested documents
- Approve recommended option
- Schedule a call
- Confirm decision
- Complete inspection
- Provide authorization

Do not bury the requested action.

---

## Step 20 — Determine Communication Sensitivity

Classify when useful:

### Routine

Important but low sensitivity.

### Elevated

Potential financial or operational impact.

### Sensitive

Coverage, claim, cancellation, client frustration, or material cost issue.

### High Stakes

Potential uninsured exposure, major claim, legal issue, coverage lapse, regulatory issue, or substantial financial impact.

Higher-sensitivity communications should receive greater human review.

---

# Human Review Escalation Standard

Require heightened human review before sending communications involving:

- Coverage denial
- Potential uninsured loss
- Cancellation
- Non-renewal
- Material agency error
- Legal dispute
- E&O concern
- Regulatory issue
- Major claim
- Client accusation
- Material financial harm
- Unauthorized coverage representation

The Skill may draft the communication.

An authorized professional should review it before release.

---

# Decision Rules

## Facts Before Reassurance

Explain what happened before attempting to reassure.

---

## Client Impact Must Be Explicit

Do not make the client determine what the situation means.

---

## Pending Is Not Confirmed

Never write a hoped-for result as though it has already happened.

---

## Process-Based Reassurance Only

Confidence should come from:

- Action
- Escalation
- Alternatives
- Timelines
- Ownership

not unsupported optimism.

---

## One Primary CTA

Make the next action obvious.

---

## Every Action Needs an Owner

Clarify who is doing what.

---

## Do Not Overload the Client

Include enough detail to make a decision.

Avoid dumping the full internal history.

---

## Do Not Hide Bad News

Clear communication builds more trust than vague language.

---

## Do Not Create Panic

Explain the difference between:

- Current impact
- Potential future impact
- Worst-case possibility

Do not present them as equivalent.

---

# Materiality / Prioritization Framework

Prioritize information using:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Client Impact | Does this materially affect the client? |
| Urgency | Does action need to happen soon? |
| Financial Impact | Could the client incur meaningful cost? |
| Coverage Impact | Could protection change? |
| Operational Impact | Could business operations be disrupted? |
| Decision Requirement | Does the client need to choose? |
| Uncertainty | Is the outcome unresolved? |
| Sensitivity | Could poor wording damage trust or create liability? |

Include what the client needs to know first.

# Failure Conditions

The Skill should not be considered complete if:

- The message is vague about what happened
- Client impact is unclear
- Pending issues are described as resolved
- Internal speculation appears in the client message
- The message provides reassurance without a plan
- No next step is identified
- Client responsibilities are vague
- Agency responsibilities are hidden
- Timing is unclear when material
- Technical language overwhelms the client
- The message minimizes legitimate concerns
- The message unnecessarily escalates fear
- Fault is admitted without authority
- Unrealistic promises are made
- A high-stakes communication is presented as ready to send without appropriate review

If any failure condition exists, revise before finalizing.

# Guardrails

## Do Not Fabricate Situation Details

Never invent:

- Carrier decisions
- Claim status
- Coverage determinations
- Premium
- Deadlines
- Renewal terms
- Escalations
- Approval status
- Client commitments
- Carrier commitments

---

## Do Not Promise Outcomes Outside the Advisor's Control

Never state:

- The carrier will approve
- The claim will be paid
- The issue will definitely be resolved
- The premium will be reduced
- Coverage will be restored

unless confirmed.

---

## Do Not Misstate Coverage

If the situation involves coverage uncertainty, distinguish:

- Known policy terms
- Carrier position
- Agency interpretation
- Pending review

Do not provide a definitive coverage conclusion without appropriate authority.

---

## Do Not Admit Fault Without Authority

Avoid statements such as:

> This was our error.

unless the issue has been confirmed and the sender is authorized to make that statement.

Appropriate language may include:

> We identified an issue in the processing and are working to correct it.

when supported.

---

## Do Not Blame the Carrier or Client

Even when another party created the problem, use professional factual language.

Avoid inflammatory or adversarial wording.

---

## Do Not Hide Material Information

Empathy should never be used to soften the message so much that the client misses the seriousness of the issue.

---

## Do Not Expose Internal Strategy

Do not unnecessarily disclose:

- E&O concerns
- Legal strategy
- Internal blame
- Employee performance concerns
- Producer compensation
- Sales strategy
- Carrier relationship politics

---

## No Legal Advice

When a situation may involve legal rights, contractual interpretation, regulatory action, or potential litigation, defer legal conclusions to appropriate counsel.

---

## Protect Confidential Information

Use only information appropriate for the intended client recipient.

Do not unnecessarily expose:

- Claimant information
- Employee-sensitive information
- Personally identifiable information
- Financial account information
- Internal agency notes
- Confidential carrier communications not intended for client release

---

## Preserve Human Accountability

This Skill supports the insurance professional.

The authorized insurance professional remains responsible for:

- Client communication
- Coverage representations
- Claims communication
- Carrier escalation
- Regulatory compliance
- Admissions of fault
- Recommendations
- Final wording
- Professional judgment

### Your people make the decisions. Your AI Teammates do the work.

# Professional Tone Standard

Write like a trusted insurance advisor communicating with a valued client during an important situation.

The tone should be:

- Clear
- Professional
- Calm
- Confident
- Empathetic
- Direct
- Accountable
- Practical
- Client-centered

Avoid:

- Corporate jargon
- Insurance jargon without explanation
- Defensive language
- Blame
- Excessive apology
- Empty reassurance
- Artificial optimism
- Overly formal legalistic language
- Overly casual language
- Vague next steps

The client should feel:

> I understand what is happening, I know what needs to happen next, and my advisor is actively managing the situation.

# Example

## Example Request

```text
Context:

Client is a regional contractor with an October 1 renewal.

The incumbent carrier has advised that it will not finalize renewal terms until the client provides updated driver information and confirms installation of telematics across all company vehicles.

The carrier has not issued a final non-renewal.

The client currently has uninterrupted coverage through September 30.

We need the driver schedule from the client by September 8.

We have escalated the renewal to the carrier's senior underwriter and are also preparing a backup market option.

The client is concerned that they may lose coverage.
```

---

## Example Output

### Suggested Subject Line

Important Update and Next Steps for Your October 1 Renewal

### Client Communication

Hi [Client Name],

I wanted to give you a clear update on where things stand with your October 1 renewal and what we need to do next.

The carrier has not declined the renewal, and your current coverage remains in place through September 30. At this point, however, the carrier will not finalize the renewal until it receives two additional items:

- An updated driver schedule
- Confirmation that telematics has been installed across the company fleet

### What This Means for You

There is no interruption in your current coverage today.

The issue is timing. We need to satisfy these underwriting requirements promptly so the carrier has enough time to complete its renewal review before October 1.

### What We Are Doing

We have already escalated the account to the carrier's senior underwriting team and are continuing to work with them on the renewal.

At the same time, we are preparing a backup market option so we are not dependent on a single outcome.

### What We Need From You

Please send us the updated driver schedule by September 8.

We will handle the carrier submission and follow-up from there.

### What Happens Next

Once we receive the driver information, we will send the complete package to underwriting immediately.

We expect to have another meaningful update after the carrier has reviewed the information, and we will update you no later than [DATE], even if the review is still in progress.

I understand that renewal uncertainty this close to the effective date can be frustrating. We are actively managing both the incumbent renewal and a backup option so we can keep the process moving and preserve alternatives.

Please send the driver schedule when available, and we will take it from there.

Best,

[Advisor Name]

# Commercial Insurance Carrier Placement Strategy

## Purpose

Develop a disciplined carrier placement and marketing strategy before submissions are released to the market.

This Skill is designed to help the insurance professional:

- Determine which types of carriers are most likely to fit the account
- Match the risk profile to appropriate market characteristics
- Prioritize quality of market access over volume of submissions
- Separate primary targets from backup markets
- Identify when standard, specialty, niche, program, or E&S markets may be appropriate
- Avoid markets that are unlikely to fit the risk
- Improve submission speed and quote success
- Position the account based on what underwriters are likely to value
- Address underwriting concerns before they become declinations
- Identify missing information before submissions are sent
- Preserve market credibility and reduce unnecessary market blocking

The objective is **not** to generate the largest possible carrier list.

The objective is to create a focused **Carrier Placement Strategy** that aligns the account with the markets most likely to understand, evaluate, and compete for the risk.

---

# Core Outcome

A successful use of this Skill should allow the broker team to answer:

- What kind of market is most appropriate for this account?
- What underwriting characteristics should the ideal carrier have?
- Which 3–5 market profiles should be approached first?
- Which backup markets should be preserved?
- Which market types are unlikely to fit?
- What makes this account desirable?
- What will concern an underwriter?
- How should the account be positioned?
- Is the submission ready to go to market?
- What information should be obtained before marketing?
- How should market access be sequenced?
- How can the broker improve quote quality rather than simply increase quote count?

The final output should create a **deliberate placement plan**, not a generic carrier brainstorm.

# Evidence Classification

Every material placement conclusion should be treated as one of the following.

## Confirmed Market Fit

Current carrier or market information supports the account characteristics.

Example:

> Current underwriting guidance indicates the market is actively seeking middle-market metal fabrication risks within this revenue range.

---

## Likely Market Fit

The market profile appears aligned based on current or recent intelligence but requires appetite confirmation.

Example:

> A regional middle-market carrier with strong manufacturing appetite may be appropriate given the account's size and favorable loss profile.

---

## Conditional Market Fit

The market may fit if one or more underwriting conditions are satisfied.

Example:

> Specialty auto markets may be viable if the client can demonstrate the newly implemented telematics and driver-management controls.

---

## Weak Market Fit

Material aspects of the risk appear inconsistent with the market profile.

Example:

> A standard preferred contractor market is unlikely to fit if more than half of operations involve high-hazard industrial work.

---

## Requires Market Confirmation

Insufficient current information exists to make a reliable carrier-specific conclusion.

---

# Confidence Classification

Where useful, classify placement conclusions:

### High Confidence

Supported by current carrier guidance or direct underwriting confirmation.

### Moderate Confidence

Supported by recent market intelligence and account fit.

### Low Confidence

Possible but dependent on meaningful appetite confirmation.

Do not create false certainty around carrier appetite.

---

# Core Principle

## Match the Risk to the Market Before Sending the Submission

Do not begin with:

> Which carriers do we have access to?

Begin with:

> What type of carrier is most likely to understand, value, and compete for this risk?

Market access alone does not equal market fit.

The goal is to align:

**Risk Profile → Carrier Appetite → Submission Positioning → Market Sequence**

## Step 5 — Determine the Appropriate Market Tier

Evaluate the account for the following broad market types.

### Preferred / Standard Market

Best suited when the account generally demonstrates:

- Stable operations
- Favorable loss experience
- Strong risk controls
- Predictable exposures
- Acceptable industry characteristics
- Adequate property protection
- Established management

---

### Middle-Market / Generalist Carrier

Appropriate when the account requires:

- Broader underwriting flexibility
- More complex multi-line placement
- Greater exposure size
- More sophisticated underwriting
- Tailored coverage structure

---

### Niche / Program Market

Appropriate when:

- The industry has a dedicated program
- Specialized forms are advantageous
- Carrier expertise improves underwriting
- Industry-specific loss control is valuable
- Program economics are competitive

---

### Specialty Admitted Market

Appropriate when:

- Risk characteristics fall outside preferred underwriting
- Specialized expertise is required
- Coverage is complex
- Higher-hazard exposures exist

---

### Excess & Surplus Lines Market

Potentially appropriate when:

- Standard markets lack appetite
- Unique exposures exist
- Capacity is constrained
- Coverage structure requires flexibility
- Loss history is challenging
- Catastrophe exposure is difficult
- High-hazard operations exist

Do not recommend E&S merely because an account is complex.

## Step 8 — Identify Secondary / Backup Markets

Preserve backup options.

Secondary markets may be:

- Less ideal but still viable
- More expensive
- More restrictive
- Specialty alternatives
- Wholesale options
- E&S alternatives
- Monoline alternatives

Do not send every backup market at the same time simply because access exists.

---

# Market Sequencing Standard

When possible, sequence markets intentionally.

### Wave 1 — Primary Targets

Best combination of:

- Appetite
- Coverage
- Price potential
- Relationship
- Expertise

### Wave 2 — Secondary Targets

Viable alternatives if primary markets:

- Decline
- Delay
- Restrict terms
- Price poorly

### Wave 3 — Contingency Markets

Specialty or E&S options preserved for difficult outcomes.

The exact sequencing should consider renewal timing.

---

## Step 9 — Identify Markets or Market Types to Avoid

Identify poor-fit markets where appropriate.

Reasons may include:

- Industry outside appetite
- Geographic restriction
- Fleet intolerance
- Catastrophe restrictions
- Loss-history intolerance
- Property-value limitations
- Unsupported umbrella limits
- Minimum-premium mismatch
- Program overlap
- Coverage incompatibility
- Historical poor results

Do not name specific carriers as “avoid” unless supported by current information.

Prefer explaining the type of market likely to waste time.

---

## Step 10 — Prevent Market Blocking

Before submission, identify:

- Existing submissions
- Wholesaler assignments
- MGA relationships
- Program exclusivity
- Direct vs. wholesale access
- Duplicate market approaches
- Prior broker activity

Do not indiscriminately send the same account through multiple channels.

Market blocking can:

- Create confusion
- Reduce broker credibility
- Complicate underwriter ownership
- Limit future access
- Damage placement strategy

---

# Market Access Governance Standard

Each market should have a clear reason for being approached.

Where applicable, record:

- Market
- Access path
- Broker / wholesaler
- Date released
- Lines requested
- Status
- Reason selected
- Exclusivity concerns

Avoid duplicate submissions unless intentionally authorized.

## Step 17 — Identify Competitive Angle

Determine what can make the submission stand out.

Potential differentiators:

- Clean and complete underwriting data
- Clear loss narrative
- Strong management story
- Meaningful corrective action
- Strong risk controls
- Broker credibility
- Clear coverage specifications
- Well-organized schedules
- Realistic timeline
- Responsive client
- Long-term account potential
- Multi-line opportunity
- Strong financial condition

The best competitive angle is usually **credibility + clarity + risk quality**, not marketing hype.

---

## Step 18 — Determine Carrier Motivation

Ask:

> Why would this market want this account?

Potential reasons:

- Attractive premium size
- Industry target
- Multi-line opportunity
- Favorable loss ratio
- Growth potential
- Geographic fit
- Strong insured controls
- Long-term relationship opportunity
- Portfolio diversification
- Cross-line opportunity

Use only factors supported by available information.

---

## Step 19 — Develop the Market Release Strategy

Define:

- Which markets go first
- Which are held in reserve
- Which lines go to which markets
- Direct vs. wholesale approach
- Target submission date
- Desired indication date
- Final quote date
- When secondary markets should be activated

The strategy should be coordinated.

---

## Step 20 — Define Stop Rules

Do not keep marketing indefinitely.

Examples of stopping conditions:

- Two or more credible primary markets provide competitive terms
- Sufficient capacity has been secured
- Additional markets are unlikely to improve outcome
- Remaining markets would create unnecessary blocking
- Client priorities are already satisfied
- Timeline no longer supports additional marketing

More market activity is not automatically better.

---

# Market Discipline Standard

The goal is not maximum submissions.

The goal is:

> The smallest number of well-selected markets required to create a credible, competitive outcome.

Before adding another market, ask:

1. What unique value could this market provide?
2. Is its appetite meaningfully different?
3. Could it improve price, coverage, capacity, or stability?
4. Does approaching it create market-blocking risk?
5. Is there enough time to evaluate the result?

If the market adds no meaningful option, do not approach it merely for volume.

---

# Decision Rules

## Quality Over Volume

Five well-selected markets are generally more valuable than fifteen poorly targeted submissions.

---

## Appetite Before Relationship

A strong carrier relationship does not make a poor-fit risk appropriate.

---

## Current Appetite Before Historical Experience

Past success with a carrier does not establish current appetite.

---

## Position the Risk Honestly

Do not hide:

- Losses
- Hazardous operations
- Catastrophe exposure
- Weak controls
- Open claims

Address them intelligently.

---

## Do Not Burn Markets

Avoid submitting incomplete accounts to high-value markets simply to “get it out.”

---

## Preserve Optionality

Do not approach every backup market immediately if the strategy benefits from staged marketing.

---

## Market Fit Is Multi-Dimensional

Evaluate:

- Industry
- Size
- Geography
- Losses
- Controls
- Coverage structure
- Capacity
- Pricing
- Service
- Long-term fit

---

## Carrier Name Requires Current Support

If current market information is unavailable, recommend a carrier profile rather than inventing a specific carrier.

# Guardrails

## Do Not Fabricate Carrier Appetite

Never invent:

- Carrier appetite
- Capacity
- Minimum premiums
- Required deductibles
- Pricing
- Underwriting restrictions
- Geographic appetite
- Program availability
- Carrier authority

If current information is unavailable, recommend a market profile and require confirmation.

---

## Do Not Guarantee Carrier Response

Never state that:

- A carrier will quote
- A carrier will bind
- A carrier will be competitive
- Capacity will be available
- Coverage will be accepted

Carrier decisions remain subject to underwriting.

---

## Do Not Misrepresent the Risk

Never conceal or materially minimize:

- Loss history
- Hazardous operations
- Open claims
- Catastrophe exposure
- Weak controls
- Product severity
- Fleet exposure
- Financial concerns

Credibility is part of placement strategy.

---

## Do Not Encourage Indiscriminate Marketing

Do not recommend approaching markets without a clear strategic reason.

---

## Do Not Create Market Blocking

Avoid duplicate or conflicting submissions where:

- Multiple wholesalers are approaching the same market
- Direct and wholesale access overlap
- Program exclusivity exists
- A market has already been reserved

Require coordination before market release.

## Example Output Excerpt

### Executive Placement Perspective

Western Industrial appears best suited to a middle-market or specialty multi-line carrier with established industrial contracting appetite and the ability to support meaningful commercial auto exposure.

The primary strength is the combination of long operating history, improving auto performance, and documented corrective fleet controls.

The primary underwriting issue will be the two significant 2024 auto losses. The placement strategy should address those losses directly and demonstrate the operational changes made since then.

A focused market approach is preferable to broad remarketing.

---

### Ideal Carrier Profile

**Market Type:**  
Middle-market admitted carrier or specialty industrial contractor market.

**Ideal Appetite Characteristics:**

- Comfortable with industrial contracting
- Able to support a 28-vehicle commercial fleet
- Values documented fleet-control improvement
- Capable of writing GL, Auto, WC, and Umbrella
- Sufficient umbrella capacity for at least $5 million
- Comfortable with Nevada and Northern California operations

---

### Primary Market Strategy

| Priority | Market Profile | Why It Fits | Likely Underwriting Focus | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Middle-market industrial contractor carrier | Strongest overall multi-line fit | Prior auto losses and fleet controls | Moderate |
| 2 | Contractor-focused program market | Industry specialization | Operations mix and subcontractor controls | Moderate |
| 3 | Specialty casualty market with fleet appetite | May tolerate historical auto severity better | Current fleet performance | Moderate |

Current carrier-specific appetite should be confirmed before assigning actual markets.

---

### Positioning Strategy

**Core Positioning Statement:**

Established industrial contractor with stable operations and an improving auto risk profile following meaningful fleet-control changes implemented after prior losses.

**Strengths to Emphasize:**

1. 18+ years of stable operations.
2. No new auto losses during the past 12 months.
3. Telematics deployed across the fleet.
4. Tightened driver qualification and MVR standards.
5. Formal subcontractor insurance controls.

**Concern to Address Proactively:**

The two significant 2024 auto losses should be presented with a concise explanation of what occurred, corrective action taken, and subsequent claim experience.

---

### Submission Readiness

**Status:** Conditionally Ready

**Missing / Unclear:**

- Updated five-year loss runs
- Current driver schedule
- Current vehicle schedule
- Confirmation of current umbrella attachment requirements

These items should be resolved before release to the highest-priority markets.

---

### Competitive Angle

The submission can stand out by presenting the account as an improving risk rather than simply a contractor with prior auto losses.

The strongest underwriting story is:

> Significant prior auto losses prompted measurable changes in fleet management, and the subsequent loss experience provides early evidence that those controls may be improving results.

---

### Recommended Placement Strategy

Approach three carefully selected primary markets first:

1. A middle-market industrial contracting carrier
2. A contractor-focused program market
3. A specialty casualty market with meaningful fleet appetite

Preserve wholesale/E&S capacity as a contingency rather than releasing the account broadly on day one.

# Commercial Insurance Post-Meeting Follow-Up

## Purpose

Transform raw meeting notes into a polished, client-ready follow-up email that reinforces value, confirms understanding, clarifies responsibilities, and maintains forward momentum in the relationship.

This Skill is designed to help the insurance professional:

- Continue the conversation naturally after a client or prospect meeting
- Show that the producer listened and understood the client’s situation
- Reinforce the most important insights from the discussion
- Translate discussion points into clear next steps
- Distinguish who is responsible for each action
- Create a professional reason for the next interaction
- Avoid vague “thank you for your time” follow-ups that do not advance the relationship
- Maintain momentum without sounding aggressive or sales-driven

The objective is **not** merely to write a polite email.

The objective is to create a follow-up communication that strengthens the relationship and makes the next action clear.

---

# Core Outcome

A successful use of this Skill should produce an email that allows the recipient to quickly understand:

- What was discussed
- What matters most
- What the advisor heard
- What was agreed
- What still needs to happen
- Who owns each next step
- What the producer recommends
- What response or action is needed from the recipient
- What happens after that action is completed

The email should feel like a natural continuation of the meeting.

# Context Interpretation Standard

Before drafting the email, classify information from the notes.

## Confirmed Discussion Point

A topic clearly discussed during the meeting.

Example:

> The client expressed concern about rising workers’ compensation costs.

---

## Agreed Action

A next step explicitly agreed during the meeting.

Example:

> The client agreed to send five years of loss runs.

---

## Producer Commitment

An action the insurance professional agreed to complete.

Example:

> The producer agreed to review the current umbrella structure.

---

## Proposed Next Step

A reasonable recommendation that was not explicitly agreed during the meeting.

Example:

> It may be helpful to schedule a separate claims review.

Clearly frame proposed next steps as recommendations rather than commitments.

---

## Producer Observation

An internal interpretation or impression.

Example:

> CFO seemed frustrated with incumbent broker communication.

Do not automatically include internal observations in the client email.

Translate them into professional, client-appropriate language only when useful.

---

# Evidence Classification

Where useful, distinguish:

## Confirmed

Directly supported by the meeting notes.

## Inferred

A likely interpretation of the discussion.

## Proposed

A recommended action not yet agreed.

Do not write inferred or proposed information as though the client already agreed to it.

---

# Core Principle

## Every Follow-Up Should Advance the Relationship

A follow-up email should do more than thank the recipient.

It should accomplish at least one of the following:

- Confirm understanding
- Reinforce an important insight
- Secure required information
- Confirm responsibilities
- Establish a next meeting
- Move the renewal process forward
- Advance an analysis
- Clarify a decision
- Create another value-producing interaction

If the email does none of these, it is probably not complete.

---

# Workflow

Follow the workflow in order unless circumstances clearly require a different sequence.

---

## Step 1 — Identify the Recipient and Relationship Context

Determine:

- Client or prospect
- Recipient name
- Recipient role
- Relationship stage
- Meeting purpose
- Level of familiarity
- Whether the communication should be more formal or conversational

The tone should reflect the actual relationship.

---

## Step 2 — Identify the Meeting Objective

Determine why the meeting occurred.

Examples:

- Understand current insurance concerns
- Prepare for renewal
- Review coverage
- Discuss claims
- Explore broker change
- Review growth
- Evaluate cost
- Discuss risk-control opportunities
- Cross-sell another line

This should influence what is emphasized in the follow-up.

---

## Step 3 — Identify the Most Important Discussion Themes

Extract the 2–5 issues that matter most.

Examples:

- Renewal pricing
- Claims performance
- Coverage gaps
- Business growth
- New locations
- Fleet risk
- Contractual requirements
- Business income
- Cybersecurity
- Carrier service
- Broker service
- Risk management
- Alternative program structure

Do not summarize every topic discussed.

Prioritize what matters.

---

## Step 4 — Identify What Matters to the Client

Ask:

> What did the client care about most?

Potential answers:

- Cost
- Stability
- Coverage
- Service
- Claims
- Compliance
- Growth
- Contract requirements
- Simplicity
- Speed
- Predictability
- Risk control

The email should reflect the client’s priorities, not merely the producer’s agenda.

---

## Step 5 — Identify Key Advisor Insights

Determine whether there are one or more useful insights worth reinforcing.

Examples:

- Premium increase appears partly exposure-driven rather than entirely rate-driven
- Loss history suggests a need for claims analysis
- Growth may have outpaced existing limits
- Current program may not reflect new operations
- Renewal timing creates an opportunity to improve leverage
- Contractual requirements should be reviewed before renewal
- Higher deductibles may deserve evaluation
- A risk-control improvement may strengthen underwriting results

Only include insights that are relevant and supportable.

Do not turn the email into a technical report.

---

## Step 6 — Separate Agreed Actions From Proposed Actions

Create two internal categories.

### Agreed

Actions specifically committed to during the meeting.

Examples:

- Client will send policies
- Producer will review loss runs
- Account Manager will request carrier alternatives
- CFO will provide updated payroll
- Next meeting will occur on a specific date

### Proposed

Actions the producer believes would be useful but were not explicitly agreed.

Examples:

- Complete business-income analysis
- Review customer contracts
- Conduct claims diagnostic
- Model deductible options

Do not present Proposed items as agreed commitments.

---

## Step 7 — Assign Action Ownership

Every material next step should identify who owns it.

Potential owners:

- Client
- Producer
- Account Manager
- Account Executive
- Claims team
- Carrier
- Loss-control consultant
- CFO
- HR
- Operations
- Legal counsel
- Other stakeholder

Avoid vague language such as:

> We’ll keep working on this.

Prefer:

> Once we receive the current policies and loss runs, our team will complete the initial coverage and claims review.

---

## Step 8 — Identify Timing

Capture any known:

- Due dates
- Renewal deadlines
- Meeting dates
- Document deadlines
- Carrier deadlines
- Internal deadlines

If timing was not agreed, do not invent a hard deadline.

Use language such as:

> When convenient, please send...

or:

> If possible, receiving these by [date] will keep us on track for the next renewal step.

only when the timing is supportable.

---

## Step 9 — Draft the Personalized Opening

The opening should:

- Thank the recipient
- Reference the meeting naturally
- Demonstrate awareness of the discussion
- Avoid generic corporate language

Weak:

> Thank you for taking the time to meet with us.

Better:

> Thanks again for the conversation today. It was helpful to better understand how the recent auto losses and upcoming renewal are affecting the decisions your team is working through.

The opening should feel specific to the conversation.

---

## Step 10 — Summarize the Discussion

Provide a concise recap.

Focus on:

- Client priorities
- Material concerns
- Relevant business changes
- Strategic themes

Avoid a transcript.

Recommended length:

- 2–4 short paragraphs, or
- A concise bullet list when multiple distinct points need confirmation

---

## Step 11 — Reinforce Key Takeaways

Identify the insights most useful to the client.

Examples:

- What appears to be driving cost
- What deserves deeper review
- Where risk may exist
- Where the program may be improved
- What needs to be validated
- What the client should be thinking about before renewal

This is where the producer demonstrates advisory value.

---

## Step 12 — Present the Next Steps

Next steps should be clear, practical, and ordered.

For each action, identify:

- What
- Who
- When, if known
- Why, when useful

Example:

> **From your team:** Send the current property schedule and five years of loss runs.

> **From our team:** Review the loss history, current limits, and available deductible alternatives.

> **Then:** We’ll reconvene to compare the available strategies before the renewal process moves further into the market.

---

## Step 13 — Build the Call to Action

The call to action should make the desired response obvious.

Potential CTAs:

- Send requested documents
- Confirm the next meeting
- Answer a specific question
- Approve a review
- Introduce another stakeholder
- Confirm priorities
- Select an option
- Provide missing data

Avoid vague endings such as:

> Let me know if you have any questions.

That may be included, but it should not be the only CTA when a specific action is needed.

---

## Step 14 — Confirm Forward Momentum

The final paragraph should establish what happens next.

Examples:

> Once we receive the policies and loss runs, we'll complete the initial review and come back with the areas we believe deserve the most attention.

or:

> With that information in hand, our next conversation can focus on comparing the incumbent strategy against the alternatives rather than starting from scratch.

The recipient should understand the sequence.

---

## Step 15 — Remove Internal Language

Before finalizing, remove or rewrite internal terminology such as:

- Wedge
- BOR strategy
- Sales opportunity
- Cross-sell target
- Incumbent weakness
- Close strategy
- Prospect qualification
- Revenue opportunity
- Pain point
- Leverage point

Unless the terminology is appropriate for the recipient.

Internal strategy notes should not leak into client-facing communication.

---

# Email Structure Standard

Unless the situation calls for another format, structure the email as:

### 1. Personalized Opening

Thank the recipient and connect naturally to the meeting.

### 2. Discussion Summary

Summarize the most important points.

### 3. Key Takeaways

Reinforce insights that matter.

### 4. Next Steps

Clarify responsibilities and timing.

### 5. Call to Action

Make the requested action clear.

### 6. Forward-Looking Close

Explain what happens after the requested action is completed.

---

# Decision Rules

## Continue the Conversation

The email should sound like the meeting just happened.

Avoid unnecessary reintroductions or background explanations the recipient already knows.

---

## Client Priorities Before Producer Priorities

Lead with what matters to the recipient.

Do not make the email primarily about:

- The agency
- The producer
- Products
- Markets
- Capabilities

unless directly relevant.

---

## Confirm Before You Expand

Do not add new recommendations that materially change the conversation without identifying them as proposed next steps.

---

## Do Not Over-Summarize

A follow-up email is not meeting minutes.

Prioritize:

- What matters
- What was learned
- What was agreed
- What happens next

---

## Do Not Over-Sell

The email should demonstrate value through understanding and action.

Avoid:

- Promotional language
- Excessive agency positioning
- Unnecessary credentials
- Generic claims of superior service
- Artificial urgency

---

## Clear Ownership

Do not write vague next steps.

Prefer:

> We'll review the loss runs.

over:

> The loss runs should be reviewed.

---

## Clear Action

If the recipient needs to do something, say exactly what.

---

## Do Not Invent Commitments

Never state:

> As agreed...

unless the notes support that an agreement actually occurred.

---

# Call-to-Action Standard

Use one primary CTA whenever possible.

Examples:

### Document CTA

> Please send the current policies and five years of loss runs when available.

### Meeting CTA

> If Tuesday at 10:00 or Thursday at 2:00 works, I can get the next review on the calendar.

### Decision CTA

> Let me know which deductible option you would like us to model first.

### Introduction CTA

> It would be helpful to include whoever oversees fleet operations in the next discussion. If you can connect us, we’ll coordinate directly.

Avoid multiple competing CTAs unless the workflow requires them.

---

# Tone Standard

The email should feel:

- Confident
- Polished
- Conversational
- Professional
- Specific
- Warm without being overly familiar
- Helpful
- Forward-moving

Avoid language that feels:

- Robotic
- Overly formal
- Overly enthusiastic
- Aggressive
- Scripted
- Sales-heavy
- Generic

---

# Length Standard

Default to concise communication.

Typical target:

### Simple Follow-Up
150–250 words

### Strategic Follow-Up
250–450 words

### Complex Multi-Issue Follow-Up
Up to approximately 600 words when necessary

Do not make the email long simply because the meeting notes are long.

Prioritize clarity over completeness.

# Failure Conditions

The Skill should not be considered complete if:

- The email could have been sent after almost any meeting
- The opening is generic
- The email simply transcribes the notes
- Important client priorities are omitted
- Agreed actions are misrepresented
- Proposed actions are presented as agreements
- The next step is unclear
- No call to action exists when one is required
- Action ownership is unclear
- Internal sales terminology appears in the client-facing email
- The message is overly promotional
- The email is unnecessarily long
- The tone feels robotic or overly formal

If any failure condition exists, revise before finalizing.

# Guardrails

## Do Not Invent Meeting Details

Never fabricate:

- Statements made by the client
- Agreements
- Decisions
- Deadlines
- Coverage details
- Premium
- Claims
- Carrier positions
- Renewal terms
- Client concerns
- Attendee commitments

---

## Do Not Misstate Agreement

Use:

> Based on our discussion...

when summarizing.

Use:

> As agreed...

only when an agreement is clearly supported by the notes.

---

## Do Not Make Unsupported Coverage Statements

Avoid statements such as:

- "You are not covered for..."
- "Your current policy will not respond..."
- "This coverage is inadequate..."

unless appropriately supported.

Prefer:

> One area we agreed deserves further review is...

or:

> We want to validate whether the current structure adequately addresses...

---

## Do Not Create False Urgency

Do not invent deadlines or pressure the recipient unnecessarily.

When timing is genuinely important, explain why.

---

## No Legal Advice

Legal, contractual, or regulatory issues may be referenced when discussed.

Do not provide unsupported legal conclusions.

---

## Protect Confidential Information

Do not unnecessarily include:

- Personally identifiable information
- Claimant information
- Sensitive employee information
- Credentials
- Financial account information
- Confidential internal agency information
- Unnecessary claim detail

Only include what is appropriate for the recipient.

---

## Preserve Human Accountability

The AI drafts the communication.

The authorized insurance professional remains responsible for:

- Final review
- Accuracy
- Client communication
- Coverage advice
- Commitments
- Deadlines
- Compliance
- Sending the message

# Professional Tone Standard

Write like an experienced commercial insurance producer who understands the client and knows what should happen next.

The tone should be:

- Professional
- Conversational
- Clear
- Confident
- Specific
- Helpful
- Client-centered
- Forward-looking

Avoid:

- Corporate filler
- Generic appreciation language
- Excessive enthusiasm
- Overly formal phrasing
- Sales clichés
- Long introductions
- Repetition
- Artificial urgency
- Robotic transitions

Prefer natural language that an experienced advisor would actually send.

# Example

## Example Request

```text
Notes:

Met with CFO Sarah and Operations Manager Mike at Western Industrial Services.

Renewal is December 1.

Client is concerned about auto pricing after two large losses from 18 months ago.

They have implemented telematics and tightened MVR standards since those losses.

No new auto claims in the last 12 months.

We discussed reviewing deductible options and giving the incumbent carrier a stronger risk-improvement story.

Sarah agreed to send updated fleet schedule and current loss runs.

We agreed that our team would review the current auto and umbrella structure and then schedule another meeting.

Need to keep the tone professional but not too formal.
```

---

## Example Output

### Suggested Subject Line

Western Industrial — Renewal Follow-Up & Next Steps

### Email

Hi Sarah,

Thank you again for the conversation today. It was helpful to better understand how the prior auto losses are affecting your renewal concerns and, more importantly, the steps your team has taken since then to improve fleet performance.

A few points stood out from our discussion. The implementation of telematics, tighter MVR standards, and the absence of new auto claims over the past 12 months give us a much stronger risk-improvement story to present to the market than the loss history alone might suggest.

We also believe it makes sense to look at the renewal from more than one angle. In addition to negotiating the incumbent carrier's position, we'll review the current auto deductible and umbrella structure to determine whether there are practical alternatives that improve the overall cost/risk balance.

As a next step:

- Your team will send the updated fleet schedule and current loss runs.
- We'll review the auto and umbrella program along with deductible alternatives.
- Once that analysis is complete, we'll reconvene and walk through the available renewal strategies before moving further into the market.

When you have the fleet schedule and loss runs available, please send them over and we'll get started.

Thanks again, Sarah. I appreciate the discussion and look forward to building the renewal strategy from here.

Best,

[Producer Name]
